How to Remove Sour Smell From Towels

Eliminate sour towel odors by washing with hot water, appropriate detergent, and odor-fighting additives safely.

Updated 2026-09-23 · Risk level 0

Quick answer

To remove sour smell from towels, launder them using the hottest water temperature safe for the fabric, following detergent label directions. Add a laundry booster like baking soda or an oxygen-based cleaner according to product instructions to neutralize odors. Avoid fabric softeners that can trap odors. After washing, dry towels thoroughly in a dryer or sun to prevent mildew. Repeat washing if the smell persists. For persistent sour odors, check towels for residual detergent or mildew and consider soaking before washing. Always follow fabric care labels and product directions to avoid damage.

What you need

  • Laundry detergent
  • Baking soda or oxygen-based laundry booster
  • Washing machine
  • Dryer or clothesline
  • Fabric care label

Step-by-step

  1. Check towel care labels

    Read the manufacturer's label on your towels to determine the highest safe water temperature and recommended washing instructions to avoid fabric damage.

  2. Use hot water and detergent properly

    Set your washing machine to the hottest water temperature safe for the towel fabric. Add detergent following the product label and your machine’s dosing instructions to effectively clean and remove bacteria causing sour odors.

  3. Add an odor-neutralizing laundry booster

    Incorporate baking soda or an oxygen-based laundry cleaner during the wash cycle according to the product label. These help break down residues and neutralize sour smells without harming fabric.

  4. Dry towels completely

    After washing, dry towels thoroughly using a machine dryer or hang them in direct sunlight. Proper drying prevents the growth of mildew and bacteria that cause sour odors.

  5. Repeat if necessary and inspect

    If sour smell remains, repeat the washing process or pre-soak towels in water mixed with an odor-neutralizing booster before washing. Inspect towels for detergent buildup or mildew spots and treat accordingly.

Why Towels Develop a Sour Smell

Towels can develop sour odors when bacteria and mildew grow due to residual moisture and detergent buildup in the fabric. Inadequate washing temperature, insufficient detergent, or improper drying contribute to this problem. Over time, oils, skin cells, and detergent residues accumulate, creating a breeding ground for odor-causing microbes.

  • Towels retain moisture and bacteria if not dried fully.
  • Detergent residues can trap odors if overdosed or rinsed poorly.
  • Washing in cold water may not kill bacteria effectively.

Choosing the Right Cleaning Agents

Using the right cleaning agents removes odor-causing elements without harming fabrics. Laundry detergents formulated to tackle odors are key, but adding baking soda or oxygen-based boosters enhances neutralization by loosening residues and balancing pH. Avoid fabric softeners as they coat fibers with residues that trap odors. Always use commercial products according to the label and appliance instructions.

  • Oxygen-based boosters are safe alternatives to bleach for whitening and deodorizing.
  • Baking soda helps neutralize odors naturally without damaging fabric.
  • Follow product directions strictly for safety and best results.

Proper Drying Techniques to Prevent Sour Smells

Complete drying is critical. Towels left damp foster microbial growth and odor. Use a dryer if possible, or hang towels outdoors in direct sunlight which helps kill bacteria naturally. Avoid storing towels when even slightly damp.

  • Use the highest safe dryer heat setting per fabric instructions.
  • Sunlight offers natural antimicrobial benefits.
  • Ensure towels are fluffy and dry before putting away.

What not to do

  • Using fabric softeners which can leave residues that trap odors
  • Washing towels in water too cool to remove bacteria
  • Leaving towels damp for extended periods, promoting mildew growth
  • Mixing bleach with vinegar or other acids, which can be hazardous

Safety notes

  • Always follow fabric care and product labels for temperature, dosing, and compatibility to avoid fabric damage.
  • Do not mix bleach with vinegar, ammonia, or other cleaners unless the manufacturer explicitly permits it.
  • Use laundry boosters only as directed on product packaging to maintain safety and effectiveness.
  • Ensure towels are completely dry before storage to prevent mold and mildew.

Frequently asked questions

Can vinegar remove sour smells from towels?

Vinegar can help neutralize odors but should never be mixed with bleach or other cleaners. Use vinegar only if product labels permit and wash thoroughly afterward.

Why do towels smell sour even after washing?

Sour smells often persist due to insufficient washing temperature, detergent buildup, or incomplete drying. Rewash towels with a laundry booster and dry fully to resolve odors.

Is it safe to use bleach on all towels to remove odors?

Bleach can damage some fabrics and colors. Check towel care labels and product guidance before using bleach, and never mix it with vinegar or ammonia-containing products.
